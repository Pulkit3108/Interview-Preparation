# Tax, Retirement, And Compensation

[Back to the finance reference](README.md)

Indian tax rules are assessment-year and regime specific. The figures below describe the Income Tax
Department's guidance for **assessment year 2026-27** and are not timeless defaults. Confirm the
current official guidance before filing or choosing an investment.

## Financial Year, Assessment Year, And ITR

The financial year is the period in which income is earned. The assessment year follows it and is
the period in which that income is assessed and the return is filed. An Income Tax Return (ITR)
reports income, eligible deductions, taxes paid, and the resulting tax or refund position.

Choose the return form from the taxpayer's actual facts. Salary amount alone is not enough: capital
gains, business income, foreign assets or income, unlisted shares, directorships, and residency can
change which form applies. Reconcile Form 16, Form 16A where relevant, Form 26AS, the Annual
Information Statement, bank interest, broker statements, and tax payments rather than copying only
the employer's calculation.

## Tax Regime Comes First

India's old and new regimes do not expose the same deductions. For AY 2026-27, the Income Tax
Department lists employer NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2) among deductions available in the
new regime, while many familiar Chapter VI-A deductions are presented under the old regime. Compare
the complete tax computation before buying a product for a deduction.

### Common Old-Regime Provisions

| Provision | What it generally covers for AY 2026-27 | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 80C, 80CCC, 80CCD(1) | Eligible payments such as provident fund, specified insurance, NSC, eligible tuition, housing-loan principal, and qualifying pension contributions | The combined listed limit is ₹1.5 lakh; eligibility and lock-ins differ by product |
| 80CCD(1B) | Eligible individual contribution to the Central Government pension scheme | Additional listed limit of ₹50,000; PRAN and contribution details are required |
| 80CCD(2) | Eligible employer contribution to NPS | Limit depends on regime and employer category; verify the current percentage rather than using the source document's old blanket figure |
| 80D | Eligible health-insurance premium, preventive check-up, and certain senior-citizen medical expenditure | Limits vary by covered person's age and whether insurance premium was paid |
| 80E | Interest on an eligible higher-education loan | Applies to eligible interest, not the entire EMI; check lender, relationship, and period conditions |
| Section 24(b) | Eligible interest on housing or improvement borrowing | Treatment differs for self-occupied and let-out property and by loan purpose/date |
| 80EE, 80EEA | Additional eligible home-loan interest in narrowly dated cases | Loan-sanction dates and other conditions matter; these are not open-ended benefits |
| 80EEB | Interest on an eligible electric-vehicle loan | AY 2026-27 guidance requires a loan sanctioned from 1 April 2019 through 31 March 2023 and lists a ₹1.5 lakh limit |
| 80GG | Eligible rent where the taxpayer is self-employed or receives no HRA | The deduction is the least of the statutory calculations and requires Form 10BA |
| 80TTA | Savings-account interest for an eligible non-senior taxpayer | AY 2026-27 guidance lists a ₹10,000 limit; this is not a blanket exemption for all deposit interest |

For AY 2026-27, the standard deduction under Section 16(ia) is ₹75,000, or salary if lower, when tax
is computed under the new regime; the otherwise applicable amount remains ₹50,000, or salary if
lower. The Income Tax Department's AY 2026-27 page lists the Section 80CCD(2) employer-NPS deduction
under the new regime at 14% of salary for all employer categories; its old-regime table lists 14%
for Central or State Government employers and 10% for PSU or other employers.

The source document's combined “employer NPS plus employer PF ₹7.5 lakh” statement must not be used
as the Section 80CCD(2) deduction limit. The deduction for an eligible employer NPS contribution and
the separate tax treatment of aggregate employer contributions are different calculations.

## Tax-Linked Products

### ELSS

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme is an equity mutual-fund category with a statutory lock-in and,
where eligible, old-regime Section 80C treatment. It still has equity-market risk. A deduction does
not guarantee capital protection or make a scheme suitable for a short-term goal.

### PPF

The Public Provident Fund is a government-backed long-term small-savings account with contribution,
tenure, withdrawal, loan, and extension rules. Interest rates and operational rules can change.
Review current official scheme rules instead of assuming monthly deposits or unrestricted access.

### EPF

The Employees' Provident Fund is an employment-linked retirement arrangement administered through
EPFO. Contributions, interest, transfer, withdrawal, nomination, and tax treatment have separate
conditions. The Universal Account Number helps consolidate and access member records; it does not
remove the need to transfer or reconcile employment records correctly.

### NPS

The National Pension System is a defined-contribution retirement system regulated by PFRDA. Returns
depend on the selected asset allocation and market performance. Tier I has retirement-oriented
withdrawal restrictions; Tier II is a separate voluntary account with different access and tax
treatment. Evaluate charges, asset mix, withdrawal/annuity rules, and tax treatment together.

## HUF

A Hindu Undivided Family can be a separate taxable person with its own PAN and return, but it is not
created merely by moving an individual's assets into a new account. Family status, source of corpus,
ownership, clubbing provisions, succession, and documentation matter. Obtain professional advice
before treating an HUF as a tax-planning mechanism.

## Equity Compensation And CTC

**Cost to company (CTC)** is the employer's stated annual cost, not guaranteed cash in hand. Split it
into fixed cash, variable cash, employer retirement contributions, insurance and benefits, one-time
items, reimbursements, and equity. Check vesting, forfeiture, repayment, and notice conditions.

**ESOP or employee stock option:** usually grants a right to buy shares later at an exercise price,
subject to vesting and plan rules. Do not confuse an option with an already-owned share.

**ESPP or employee stock purchase plan:** lets eligible employees buy employer shares, sometimes at
a discount or using payroll deductions. Look for offering periods, purchase limits, holding rules,
currency conversion, brokerage, and tax events.

**RSU or restricted stock unit:** is a promise to deliver shares or cash after vesting conditions are
met. It normally has no exercise price, but vesting, settlement, withholding, and sale can create
different tax and cash-flow events.

For all employer equity, evaluate concentration: salary, career prospects, and investment value can
all depend on the same company. Also check whether the shares are listed, when they can be sold,
foreign-asset reporting, valuation, and what happens on resignation or termination.

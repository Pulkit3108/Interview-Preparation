## Return Measures

**CAGR** is the constant annualized rate that links a beginning value to an ending value over a
period:

```text
CAGR = (ending value / beginning value)^(1 / years) - 1
```

It smooths the path and does not show volatility, interim losses, cash-flow timing, taxes, or fees.

**XIRR** estimates an annualized internal rate of return for irregular dated cash flows. It is useful
for SIPs and portfolios with purchases, withdrawals, or distributions on different dates. The result
depends on complete, correctly signed cash flows and can be ambiguous in unusual cash-flow patterns.

**Rolling returns** are returns for many overlapping windows of equal length. For example, a
three-year rolling series can calculate annualized return for every possible three-year window over
the observation period. The source document's formula that averages yearly percentage returns is
not a rolling-return calculation and ignores compounding.

**Tracking difference** is the fund return minus benchmark return over a period. **Tracking error**
measures variability of active return, commonly the annualized standard deviation of periodic fund
return minus benchmark return. A fund can have low tracking error but a persistent negative tracking
difference due to costs.

## Trading Styles

**Paper trading** simulates orders without real capital. It is useful for learning mechanics but may
omit slippage, partial fills, latency, market impact, fees, taxes, borrow constraints, and emotional
pressure.

**Intraday trading** opens and closes positions within the trading day. **Swing trading** generally
holds positions for days or weeks. Both expose the trader to execution, liquidity, gap, model, and
behavioral risk; neither creates an expected profit merely by shortening the holding period.

**Futures** create obligations under standardized derivative contracts. **Options** give the buyer a
right and impose an obligation on the writer under contract terms. Payoff depends on direction,
strike, premium, time, volatility, contract multiplier, margin, and settlement. Leverage can create
losses much larger than the initial cash committed, especially for option writers and futures
positions. Learn contract specifications and risk controls before trading.

## A Safer Company-Research Process

The source recommended buying at most one or two mid- or small-cap companies. That is concentration,
not diversification. It also suggested that paying taxes and dividends proves a small company has
cash. Those payments are evidence to reconcile, not proof that accounts are reliable.

A more defensible research sequence is:

1. understand the business model, customers, competitive position, industry structure, and major
   risks;
2. reconcile revenue, profit, operating cash flow, working capital, capital expenditure, debt, and
   cash over several periods;
3. read accounting policies, auditor qualifications, related-party transactions, contingent
   liabilities, promoter pledges, dilution, and governance disclosures;
4. compare margins, growth, returns on capital, and valuation with relevant peers while explaining
   differences;
5. test downside scenarios for demand, pricing, funding, regulation, currency, and key-person risk;
6. decide position size from portfolio-level risk and liquidity, not conviction alone; and
7. document the thesis, disconfirming evidence, valuation range, review triggers, and exit logic.

Sales growth above an industry average can indicate share gains, acquisition, price increases, or a
different product mix. Gross margin can reflect pricing power but also accounting classification.
Cash flow, tax paid, and dividends should be reconciled with balance-sheet movements and disclosures.

## Personal-Finance Rules In Context

The rule of 72, age-based equity allocation, 50-30-20 budgeting, pay-yourself-first, six-month
emergency reserve, auto-sweep, income multiples for life cover, EMI-to-income limits, and 25-times
expenses retirement rule are all preserved in the
[career-transition finance guide](../personal-finance-for-career-transitions.md). They are screening
heuristics, not guarantees or eligibility rules.

The source's “3x3x3” list is better treated as a checklist, not a mandatory product allocation:

- protection: appropriate life and health cover plus an emergency reserve;
- obligations: debt-repayment plan and goal-specific retirement planning;
- growth: diversified investments selected by goal, horizon, risk, cost, liquidity, and tax.

NPS, gold, real estate, mutual funds, or individual stocks may or may not belong in a particular
plan. Product choice follows the need; it does not define it.

# Funds And Investment Products

[Back to the finance reference](README.md)

## Pooled-Investment Vocabulary

A **mutual fund** pools investors' money into a portfolio operated under a scheme mandate. Investors
own units, not the portfolio's individual securities. An **asset management company (AMC)** manages
schemes subject to the governing documents and regulation. **AMFI** is the Indian mutual-fund
industry association; SEBI is the regulator.

**Net asset value (NAV)** is scheme assets minus liabilities, divided by outstanding units under the
applicable valuation rules. NAV being ₹10 or ₹100 does not make one fund cheaper than another.
**Assets under management (AUM)** measures managed asset value, not return quality or safety.

A **systematic investment plan (SIP)** schedules recurring purchases of fund units. It supports
discipline and spreads purchase dates but does not guarantee a profit or protect capital. **PMS** is
a separately managed portfolio service in which the client generally owns the underlying
securities; minimums, fees, concentration, discretion, custody, and tax reporting differ from a
mutual fund.

## Fund And Index Types

- **Index fund:** seeks to track a specified index before costs. Examine the exact index,
  replication method, tracking difference, tracking error, fees, and liquidity.
- **ETF:** pooled security traded on an exchange. Market price can differ from NAV, so examine bid-ask
  spread, trading volume, market makers, creation/redemption, and brokerage in addition to expense.
- **Large-, mid-, and small-cap funds:** invest according to the regulator's current market-cap
  definitions and scheme limits. Smaller companies are not automatically better growth investments
  and can carry greater liquidity, governance, and volatility risk.
- **Flexi-cap fund:** can allocate across market-cap segments within its mandate.
- **Multi-cap fund:** follows regulator-specified minimum allocation across large-, mid-, and
  small-cap segments. It is not inherently better than flexi-cap; the constraint and goal differ.
- **Debt fund:** holds debt and money-market instruments. Returns can be affected by interest rates,
  duration, credit quality, liquidity, and defaults; it is not equivalent to a guaranteed deposit.
- **Money-market fund:** invests in eligible short-maturity money-market instruments under its
  mandate. Low duration does not mean zero credit or liquidity risk.
- **Arbitrage fund:** seeks price differences across markets while hedging corresponding exposure.
  Spreads, execution, costs, liquidity, and tax treatment affect outcomes.
- **Balanced-advantage or dynamic-asset-allocation fund:** varies allocation among asset classes
  using the scheme's model and discretion. “Balanced” does not guarantee low loss.
- **Feeder fund:** invests substantially in another fund. Add both layers' fees, liquidity,
  currency, jurisdiction, and tax effects.
- **Blue-chip fund:** often a marketing description rather than a sufficient risk definition. Read
  the regulated scheme category and portfolio instead of relying on the label.

Scheme categorization changes. Use the latest SEBI circular and scheme information document, not a
saved definition alone.

## Common Indexes

**Nifty 50**, **Nifty Next 50**, and **Sensex** are different Indian equity indexes with published
eligibility and weighting methods. **S&P 500** represents a broad set of large US companies under
S&P's methodology. **Nasdaq-100** tracks large non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq under its
methodology. None is “the market” in full; country, sector, currency, valuation, and concentration
exposures differ.

## ETF Due Diligence

ETF names, mandates, fees, availability, and portfolios can change. For any ETF, verify:

1. legal name, exchange symbol, AMC, current scheme documents, and benchmark;
2. what the index actually holds and how it weights and rebalances constituents;
3. expense ratio, tracking difference, tracking error, spread, volume, and premium/discount to NAV;
4. replication method, securities lending, derivatives, currency exposure, and counterparty risk;
5. distribution policy, tax treatment, and whether dividends are paid or reinvested; and
6. overlap with the rest of the portfolio.

## Other Investment Products

**Bond or debt instrument:** a contractual obligation under which an issuer borrows and promises
specified payments. Government, municipal, and corporate issuers have different credit, interest-
rate, liquidity, reinvestment, call, currency, and inflation risks. Bonds are not universally
“high-security.”

**Treasury bill:** a short-term Government of India security issued at a discount and redeemed at
face value. Market price can move before maturity. Access, auction, settlement, and tax treatment
depend on the route used.

**REIT:** a regulated pooled vehicle holding qualifying real-estate assets and distributing income
under its framework. It carries property, tenant, leverage, rate, valuation, governance, and market
liquidity risk and is not the same as directly owning a home.

**Sovereign Gold Bond:** a government security denominated in grams of gold under the terms of a
specific issuance. Interest, maturity, redemption windows, secondary-market liquidity, and tax
treatment are issue- and law-specific. Do not assume a fresh tranche is always available.

**Gold ETF, digital gold, and recurring gold plan:** these are different structures. Verify who owns
and custodies the metal, purity, audit, insurance, redemption, spread, fees, legal protections, and
regulator. “Digital” access alone does not establish equivalent protection.

**P2P lending:** an RBI-regulated NBFC-P2P platform facilitates loans between participants; it does
not turn the loans into bank deposits or guarantee repayment. Evaluate borrower defaults,
diversification, platform failure, servicing, recovery, liquidity, fees, and current exposure caps.

**Chit fund:** a rotating savings and credit arrangement governed by its legal structure and rules.
It is neither a mutual fund nor automatically equivalent to a regulated bank deposit. Verify
registration, organizer, auction mechanics, subscriber obligations, defaults, security, and state
oversight.

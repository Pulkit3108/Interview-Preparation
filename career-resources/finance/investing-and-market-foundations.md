# Investing And Market Foundations

## Start With The Goal

Define the amount, time horizon, required liquidity, loss tolerance, and consequence of missing the
goal before choosing a product. Diversification reduces dependence on one company, sector, issuer,
country, currency, or asset class; it does not eliminate loss.

**Growth investing** emphasizes businesses expected to grow revenue or earnings faster than a
reference market. Expectations may already be reflected in the price. **Value investing** looks for
securities priced below an estimate of fundamental value. That estimate can be wrong, and a low
multiple can reflect a deteriorating business. Neither label is a complete investment process.

## Market Infrastructure In India

- **SEBI** regulates India's securities market and works to protect investors and develop the market.
- A **stock exchange**, such as NSE or BSE, provides a regulated market for eligible securities.
- **NSDL** and **CDSL** are securities depositories. Depository participants connect investors to a
  depository. It is misleading to reduce NSDL to “the NSE depository” and CDSL to “the BSE
  depository”; investors and market intermediaries can connect across market infrastructure.
- A **clearing corporation** becomes the central counterparty for eligible trades and manages
  clearing, settlement, collateral, and default risk. Indian Clearing Corporation is associated with
  BSE; NSE Clearing was formerly called National Securities Clearing Corporation.
- A **broker** executes orders and provides market access. Verify SEBI registration, charges,
  custody arrangements, grievance mechanisms, and permissions before funding an account.

A **stock** represents corporate equity; a **share** is a unit of that ownership. An **IPO** is the
first public offering of specified shares by an issuer. IPO allocation or listing gains are not
guaranteed; read the offer document, risks, use of proceeds, capital structure, and valuation.

## Orders And Risk Controls

A **stop order** triggers when its condition is reached; execution price may differ from the trigger,
especially in a fast or illiquid market. A stop-limit order can control price but may not execute.
Neither replaces position sizing, diversification, or an exit thesis.

## Reading Company Measures

| Measure | Simplified calculation | What it can and cannot tell you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P/E | Market price per share / earnings per share | Relates price to accounting earnings; distorted by losses, cyclicality, one-offs, and accounting choices |
| P/B | Market price per share / book value per share | Relates market value to accounting net assets; relevance varies by sector and asset quality |
| Current ratio | Current assets / current liabilities | Snapshot of short-term balance-sheet coverage; says little about asset quality or timing by itself |
| Debt ratio | Define explicitly, often total debt / total assets | Indicates leverage only under the chosen definition; compare like with like |
| EBITDA | Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization | Operating-performance proxy, not cash flow; omits capex, working capital, financing, and tax cash flows |
| Dividend yield | Annual dividend per share / market price per share | Historical or indicated income relative to price; dividends can be changed or cancelled |

Ratios require trends, peer context, footnotes, segment economics, accounting quality, and cash-flow
reconciliation. A single low or high ratio is not a buy signal.

## Capital Gains Holding Period

Long-term capital gain is a tax classification, not simply “any asset held over 12 months.” Holding
periods and rates depend on asset type, listing status, transaction date, and current law. The Income
Tax Department's ITR guidance, for example, distinguishes listed shares and specified securities,
unlisted shares, and immovable property. Verify the rule for the exact asset and year.

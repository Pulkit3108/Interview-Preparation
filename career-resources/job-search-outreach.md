# Job Search Outreach

Reusable, truthful templates for contacting recruiters, hiring managers, and potential referrers.

[Back to career resources](README.md)

## Before Sending A Message

Identify a specific role or team and confirm that your strongest evidence matches its requirements.
Keep the request easy to evaluate: state the role, provide the job identifier or link, summarize two
or three relevant qualifications, and include one reliable resume or portfolio link.

Do not claim experience, awards, projects, or relationships you do not have. Avoid sending sensitive
identity documents, compensation records, or confidential work samples during initial outreach.

## Recruiter Email For A Posted Role

**Subject:** Application for `[role]` - `[job identifier]`

```text
Hello [name],

I am applying for the [role] position, [job identifier]. My experience with [relevant skill or
outcome] and [relevant skill or outcome] aligns with the role's focus on [specific requirement].

Resume: [link]
Portfolio or relevant work: [link, if useful]

I would be glad to discuss how my experience could help the team. Thank you for your time.

Regards,
[name]
```

Replace generic skill lists with evidence. “Reduced processing time by `[measured result]` while
owning `[scope]`” is stronger than “hard-working expert in many technologies.”

## Referral Request

Ask for an informed referral, not merely access to someone's employer.

```text
Hello [name],

I am considering [role], [job identifier], at [company]. My background in [relevant area] appears to
match [specific responsibility]. I have included my resume and the job link below.

If you know the team or role well enough to assess the fit, would you be comfortable referring me?
No problem if you do not have enough context.

Job: [link]
Resume: [link]

Thank you,
[name]
```

Give the recipient a comfortable way to decline. Never imply that accepting a connection request
creates an obligation to refer you.

## Message To Learn About A Role

```text
Hello [name],

I am researching the [role] position on [team or product]. Your experience with [specific area]
stood out to me. If you have time, could you share one or two things that distinguish the team's
day-to-day work from the job description?

I understand if you are unable to discuss internal details. Thank you for considering the request.
```

Ask a narrow question that the person's public experience makes them suited to answer. Do not ask
for confidential plans, interview questions, or internal hiring decisions.

## Reply To A Recruiter

```text
Hello [name],

Thank you for contacting me about the [role] opportunity. I am interested in learning more about the
team, role scope, level, location or working model, and interview process.

My current resume is attached or available here: [link]. Please let me know the next step and any
information you need from me.

Regards,
[name]
```

Before sharing current compensation or documents, understand why they are requested, what local law
permits, and how the recipient will protect the information.

## Cover Letter Structure

A useful cover letter is short and evidence-led:

1. Name the role and explain the specific reason for applying.
2. Connect one or two requirements to relevant accomplishments.
3. Explain why the team, product, or problem is a meaningful fit.
4. Close with a direct invitation to discuss the role.

Do not repeat the entire resume or use a generic biography. Tailor claims to the role and remove
employer-confidential details.

## Follow Up

If no timeline was provided, one concise follow-up after several business days is normally enough.
Reference the original conversation and provide any requested material. Repeated daily messages,
artificial urgency, or contacting unrelated employees can harm the application.
